TM44 Inspection Requirements & Regulations Guide

A straightforward guide to air-conditioning inspection requirements, the 12kW threshold, inspection frequency, reports and what building operators need to know.

What is a TM44 inspection?

TM44 is the CIBSE methodology commonly used for statutory energy-efficiency inspections of air-conditioning systems. The inspection considers accessible equipment, controls, maintenance information and system operation, and provides recommendations that may improve energy efficiency.

Who needs an inspection?

In England and Wales, air-conditioning systems with an effective rated output of more than 12kW fall within the Energy Performance of Buildings inspection requirements. Where several air-conditioning units are under the control of the same person, their combined capacity can be relevant when establishing whether the threshold is exceeded.

UK note: England & Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland do not operate under identical rules. Multi-site estates spanning several UK nations should be checked against the requirements applying in each jurisdiction.

How often is an inspection required?

In England and Wales, qualifying systems are inspected at intervals not exceeding five years. Northern Ireland also requires periodic inspections. Scotland has its own regulatory framework and inspection arrangements.

What does the inspection cover?

  • Accessible air-conditioning equipment and its general condition
  • Control systems, time schedules and set points
  • Available maintenance and system documentation
  • Operational characteristics that may affect efficiency
  • Recommendations for improving system operation or energy performance

What do you receive?

Following the inspection, the relevant report and compliance record are produced and, where required by the applicable scheme, lodged on the relevant register. The recommendations help the system operator understand possible improvements and areas that may merit further investigation.

Who can carry out a TM44 inspection?

Inspections must be undertaken by appropriately qualified or accredited assessors under the rules applying in the relevant part of the UK. The required assessor level depends on the type and complexity of the air-conditioning system.

What about multi-site estates?

For a large estate, the difficult part is often not the individual survey but managing certificate dates, access, scheduling, site changes, report production and completion across hundreds of locations. A successful programme needs a reliable site register and a clear status for every property.

How should I prepare?

Where available, asset lists, previous reports, maintenance records, O&M information, drawings and suitable access to plant and controls can make the inspection more efficient. For complex or secure sites, access requirements should be confirmed before attendance.

Important: This guide is general information rather than legal advice. Regulations and government guidance can change and differ across the UK, so the current requirements should be checked for a particular site or programme.
